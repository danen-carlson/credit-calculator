Best Credit Cards for Streaming & Subscriptions (2026)

The average American pays for 4–5 streaming subscriptions totaling $50–80/month ($600–960/year). With the right card, that's $36–58/year in cash back on money you're already spending. The Amex Blue Cash Preferred leads with 6% on streaming, but several no-AF options earn 3–5%. Use our calculator to find your best match.

Important: Not all subscriptions code as "streaming." Amazon Prime membership codes as retail, Apple services require Apple Pay for 3%, and gym memberships code differently than media subscriptions. We break down what counts below.

What Counts as "Streaming"

Not all subscriptions are created equal in the eyes of credit card issuers. Here's what codes as streaming vs. what doesn't:

  • ✅ Qualifies as streaming: Netflix, Hulu, Disney+, ESPN+, Peacock, HBO Max, Paramount+, Apple TV+ (standalone), Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube Premium, YouTube TV, Sling, SiriusXM, Pandora
  • ❌ Does NOT qualify: Amazon Prime membership (codes as retail), gym memberships (codes as health/fitness), newspaper subscriptions (codes as publishing), meal kits (codes as food), cloud storage (codes as business services)
  • ⚠️ Varies by issuer: Amazon Prime Video standalone ($8.99/mo) sometimes codes as streaming. Apple services via Apple Pay get 3% on Apple Card regardless of category.

The Multi-Card Strategy

Since no single card maximizes every subscription, consider splitting your subs across cards:

  • Amex Blue Cash Preferred (6%) → Netflix, Hulu, Disney+, Spotify, SiriusXM
  • Amazon Prime Visa (5%) → Amazon Prime, Prime Video, Audible
  • Apple Card (3% via Apple Pay) → Apple Music, Apple TV+, Apple Arcade, iCloud+
  • Citi Double Cash (2%) → Everything else (gym, meal kits, newspapers)

This multi-card approach sounds tedious, but it takes 10 minutes to set up once and earns you $50–80/year on subscriptions you'd pay for anyway. Set each subscription to auto-pay on the right card and forget about it.

The Citi Custom Cash Trap

The Citi Custom Cash gives 5% on your top spending category each month. If you spend more on groceries ($400) than streaming ($80), streaming won't be your top category — and you'll only earn 1%. The card works great for streaming-heavy months (like when you add a new service), but it's unreliable as a dedicated streaming card unless streaming consistently dominates your spending.
