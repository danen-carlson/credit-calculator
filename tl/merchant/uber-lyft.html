How Rideshare Coding Works

Both Uber and Lyft transactions almost always code as travel with credit card issuers. This means any travel-category card earns bonus points on rideshare — not just dedicated rideshare cards. Here's what each platform codes as:

  • Uber rides → Travel (with most issuers)
  • Uber Eats → Restaurant/dining (NOT travel)
  • Lyft rides → Travel (with most issuers)
  • Lyft delivery → Varies by issuer

This distinction matters: a travel card like Chase Sapphire Preferred earns 3x on Uber rides but only 1x on Uber Eats. For food delivery, you want a dining card (Amex Gold at 4x) instead.

Uber vs Lyft: Which Card Wins?

The Barclays Uber Pro Card only earns bonuses on Uber — not Lyft. If you split between platforms or prefer Lyft, consider:

  • Lyft-only users: Chase Sapphire Preferred (3x travel) or Capital One Venture X (2x everything)
  • Both platforms: Amex Gold covers both Uber Eats and restaurant delivery regardless of platform
  • Uber loyalists: Uber Pro Card at 7% is unbeatable
