❓ Frequently asked questions

Is the Amex Blue Cash Preferred worth the $95 annual fee?
If you spend $400+/month at U.S. supermarkets, yes. At $400/mo, you earn $288/year in grocery cash back alone (6% on $4,800). Subtract the $95 AF and you're netting $193. Add gas/transit/streaming rewards and you're well into positive territory. Below $150/mo in groceries, the math flips — consider the no-AF Blue Cash Everyday instead.
What counts as a "U.S. supermarket" for the 6%?
Standalone grocery stores like Kroger, Safeway, Publix, Whole Foods, and Trader Joe's. Superstores like Walmart, Target, Costco, and Sam's Club do NOT count. The $6,000 annual cap applies to the grocery category; after that, groceries earn 1%.
How does the streaming 6% cash back work?
You earn 6% on eligible U.S. streaming subscriptions: Netflix, Hulu, Spotify, Disney+, Amazon Prime, Apple TV+, and more. There's no annual cap on streaming. This alone can net $50–$100/year if you have 3–5 subscriptions.
Is the Blue Cash Preferred better than the Amex Gold for groceries?
BCP gives straight 6% cash back on up to $6K/yr. Gold gives 4x points on up to $25K/yr. If you value Amex points at 1.5¢ each, Gold gives an effective 6% but only if you transfer points to partners. For pure cash back in your pocket? BCP wins. For travel rewards? Gold wins.
What happens after the $6,000 grocery cap?
Grocery spend above $6,000/year drops from 6% to 1%. If you spend more than $500/month on groceries, track your spend — beyond $6K, consider switching to a card like the Citi Custom Cash for the overage.
Does the Blue Cash Preferred have foreign transaction fees?
No — Amex removed the foreign transaction fee on BCP. You can use it abroad for groceries and earn 1% (international supermarkets won't code as U.S. supermarkets for the 6% rate).
